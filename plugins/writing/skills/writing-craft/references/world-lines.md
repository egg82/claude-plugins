# Short-form world and story lines

Item and lore text, in-fiction journals and records, flavour text, vignettes and flash fiction, and the
worldbuilding behind them. The line rules also serve narration in a longer scene or story. A game's event log is
UI text (`game-ui-text.md`). Most sources are dark and weird fantasy, horror and games; where a rule holds only
there, it says so. These are fiction rules: instructions, help and informational text say the last step outright
(`plain-language.md`). Per-genre conventions are in `story-beats.md`.

## Line rules

- **Cap length hard, per kind of line.** Failbetter caps the parts of a storylet at 30, 20 and 100 words, and
  replaces summary phrases ("All across the city...") with one person, place or act (practitioner, secondhand
  via a summarizer, https://www.failbettergames.com/fallen-london-writer-guidelines-part-iii/). Set the cap where
  the kind of line is defined, and keep it.
- **One vivid concrete detail, then stop.** One image implies the room; don't describe its surroundings, and don't
  write the connective tissue between beats: "the player's imagination is your ally" (practitioner, Failbetter,
  http://www.failbettergames.com/narrative-snippets-parsimony-2/ and
  http://www.failbettergames.com/points-of-light-pools-of-shadow-part-i/).
- **Show the trace a feeling leaves.** Make a state visible through what can be seen. Show the aftermath (an object,
  its state, where it lies) and let the reader infer the event (practitioner, Failbetter; Smith and Worch, "What
  Happened Here?", GDC 2010, snippet, https://gdcvault.com/play/1012647/What-Happened-Here-Environmental). Time and
  mood come through what people are doing, more than through light and weather.
- **Name odd concrete things and don't explain them.** "molluscs from the slime banks", "city of fallen pylons":
  ordinary goods, food and trade imply a world better than history does. Unexplained works only when the detail is
  physically specific (opinion, on Jack Vance,
  https://interestingliterature.com/2020/01/jack-vance-dying-earth-review/). Don't volunteer history; let the
  reader dig (opinion, on Kentucky Route Zero, snippet,
  https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/welcome-to-the-zero-the-making-of-kentucky-route-zeros-open-road).
- **Omit only what you know.** A line may leave out what the writer could state, because a private canon sits
  behind it. A line that withholds with nothing behind it is vagueness (practitioner, Hemingway's iceberg,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceberg_theory). So write the fact down somewhere before you leave it out.
- **Details do work; they never survey.** No line after line of setting, and no details that fit together into a
  neat catalogue (opinion, M. John Harrison via https://3quarksdaily.com/?p=139062).
- **The swap test.** Change a few nouns. If the line reads the same in another setting, it is generic (opinion, Le
  Guin, "From Elfland to Poughkeepsie", snippet,
  https://jmbaldwinwriter.substack.com/p/thoughts-on-from-elfland-to-poughkeepsie). In contemporary or realist
  fiction the test is whether it could be any town and any year, not whether it sounds modern.
- **One register.** Le Guin's objection is to register that wobbles; the excerpt does not settle plain against
  elevated (opinion, same source). In a secondary world that means no modern idiom or managerial vocabulary in the
  world's voice; contemporary fiction is modern and uses modern idiom.
- **Plain narration, period flavour in a few touches.** Unobtrusive words ("shouts, not vociferises") and a few
  deliberate period details, never pastiche of the period's prose (developer, Failbetter,
  http://www.failbettergames.com/points-of-light-pools-of-shadow-part-ii/).
- **End lore, vignettes and flash fiction on a turn, never an aphoristic close.** A turn is a concrete thing the
  reader completes inside the world; a maxim that would hold outside it is the measured giveaway (definition and
  evidence: `llm-tells.md`, the turn). A plain record with no turn suits an event log; informational text states
  its point.
- **Item text points at a story and leaves the stat to the UI:** an origin, a former owner or an event, in a line or
  two (opinion, snippet, https://flarerpg.org/2013/02/07/20130207/; Dark Souls item text is the published model).

## Worldbuilding method

Method for a coherent invented world, for speculative fiction and games; the canon rule at the end holds for any
long story. The evidence is mostly secondhand (fan analysis, trade press).

- **Follow one premise's consequences everywhere.** Annihilation's Shimmer applies one rule to everything inside it,
  so its creatures are consequences of a rule rather than a zoo (practitioner,
  https://vfxvoice.com/an-altered-realm-of-being-and-beings-haunt-annihilation/). Dixon's *After Man* explains a
  whole fauna from one premise (practitioner, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After_Man).
- **Build bottom-up: terrain and climate, then ecology, then economy, then politics.** Ask what the staple crops,
  herds and predators are, what the land can trade, how food is processed and not only named, and what everyday
  objects are made of; don't import potatoes by default. How changes link matters more than realism (practitioner,
  Jenn Lyons, https://reactormag.com/how-to-make-an-apple-pie-ecologies-and-economies-in-sff/).
- **Inner consistency is what readers believe.** A secondary world holds while it keeps its own laws, and "the
  moment disbelief arises, the spell is broken" (opinion, Tolkien, "On Fairy-Stories", via
  https://gwern.net/doc/fiction/2015-mirante-thesubcreationtheoryofjrrtolkien.html).
- **Give every premise-driven change a second-order effect** on neighbours, habitat or the people nearby
  (practitioner, speculative zoology,
  https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/tetrapod-zoology/speculative-zoology-a-discussion/).
- **Explain presence and absence.** Draw the food web or supply chain before the catalogue: who eats whom, who
  feeds whom, why a thing is here and not there (practitioner, Monster Hunter: World's director,
  https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/6549/creating-a-dense-open-world-a-lecture-from-yuya-tokuda-the-director-for-monster-hunter-world).
- **Group by shared cause** so the reader learns a rule and applies it; tie each thing to a human consequence (a
  spoiled crop, a closed road, a story told about it).
- **Escalate by kind.** The next region asks a different question; a bigger number on the same thing reads as more
  of the same (judgement).
- **Familiar with one wrong detail** unsettles more than the wholly alien: recognisable animals with an off feature
  (opinion, snippet, reviews of Annihilation). Miyazaki asked for "the deep sorrow of a magnificent beast" over
  disgust (practitioner, https://killscreen.com/hidetaka-miyazakis-transcendent-quest-beauty-bloodborne).
- **A field-note register** (journals, bestiaries, logs kept by a character): date, place and how the thing was
  found, sign first and creature second; separate seen from inferred; prefer measured things to adjectives. In
  horror a stated gap ("not observed feeding") does the work of dread (practitioner, the Grinnell method,
  https://mvz.berkeley.edu/the-grinnell-method). SCP's clinical register works the same way, by negative space
  (practitioner, https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/you-already-know-what-the-fuck-is-going-on).
- **Keep canon in keyed records, never in rereading prose.** Model rereading of long stories catches continuity
  errors at about 0.60 against 0.50 chance, and model rewrites add plot holes at 1.5 to 2 times the original's rate
  (measured, Ahuja et al. 2025, "Finding Flawed Fictions", https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11900). Studios keep a canon
  database or continuity bible, one fact per entry with its source (practitioner, secondhand, Lucasfilm's Holocron,
  https://starwars.archivum.wiki/article/Leland_Chee).

How characters talk is in `dialogue.md`.
