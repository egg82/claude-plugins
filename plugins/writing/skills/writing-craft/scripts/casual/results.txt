CASUAL REGISTER: HN human replies vs Claude replies

Question: is Claude's casual writing "too well-educated" (wider vocabulary, tidier capitalisation and punctuation,
no drift within a message)?

Answer in one paragraph. On this sample the vocabulary half is not supported: word variety (MATTR), the share of
uncommon words and contraction use do not differ clearly between HN humans and Claude. The tidiness half is
supported, with a small absolute size because HN commenters are themselves tidy: Claude almost never starts a
sentence lowercase (0.25% vs 2.5%), never ends a message without terminal punctuation (0% vs 10%), never gets
sloppier from the first sentence to the last (0% vs 9.7% of 3+ sentence messages), uses fewer semicolons, and varies
sentence length less (SD 6.4 vs 9.4 words). HN is a careful forum, so the baseline understates how casual casual
can be.

Method and data: human.jsonl (100 HN reply comments, 2015-2019, 25-150 words, parent a comment, seeded sampling
by fetch_hn.py), claude.jsonl (one reply per parent, written before seeing the human reply, each within +-20% of the
human reply's word count; build_claude.py joins replies/*.txt), measure.py (stdlib; run with --results to rebuild
this file).

How to read the table: HN and Claude are the pooled values (ratios of sums, or means over messages where
the metric is per message). Each difference has a bootstrap 95% interval. "CLEAR" means the interval excludes 0;
"not clear" means it does not, which is not evidence of no difference at n=100.

Hand check of non-dictionary tokens (20 sampled per side, seed 7; classified by reading each token):
  HN human: 2 of 20 flagged tokens were real typos or garbles ('thosed', 'dift'); 18 were product names, brand names, identifiers or newer words missing from the dictionary (Uber, FreeBSD, jemalloc, website, blogs).
  Claude: 0 of 20 were typos; all were product names, newer words or jargon (ptrace, Mavic, metadata, toolchain, website).
  reading: Samples are 20 per side, so the typo shares (about 10% and 0%) carry wide error; they say the dictionary flag is mostly jargon, and that real typos are rare on both sides. Not a measured difference.


WHICH DIFFERENCES ARE CLEAR (Claude minus HN, 95% interval excludes 0)
  lower:  sentences starting lowercase (-2.3 points), messages ending without . ! ? (-10 points), messages where the
          first sentence is more formal than the last (-9.7 points), sentence-length SD (-2.9 words), dictionary
          misses (-0.8 per 100 words; mostly Claude using fewer product names, see hand check, not a vocabulary
          finding), semicolons (-0.10 per 100 words; the interval's upper end is -0.00, so borderline).
  Seventeen metrics were tested, so one or two of the six could be chance; the first four are the solid ones.
WHICH ARE NOT CLEAR
  MATTR-25, uncommon-word share, LLM-list words (zero in both groups, so the list does not fire on short
  replies; the counter returns non-zero on a control string), lowercase "i" (zero in both), em dashes,
  colons, apostrophe drops, contractions (Claude is higher by 0.4 per 100 words, interval spans 0), acronyms in both
  cases (zero in both), the mean drift score.

LIMITS
  - HN is a tech forum, more careful than texts or Discord. HN humans start 97.5% of sentences capitalised, so
    gaps there are small; a looser casual register would show larger gaps.
  - One model wrote all Claude replies, in one session, knowing the task was a comparison. It wrote them from the
    parent text alone and saw only the human reply's word count. Length matching forced several trims and extensions,
    which can distort how a reply ends. Thread context beyond the parent was not available to either side.
  - "Outside the 2,000 most frequent words" was not built as stated: no frequency list exists on this host, so
    the common set is the embedded list plus HN parent-comment words seen twice or more, 828 words in total. The
    share is relative to that set and its level is not comparable with other studies; the HN-vs-Claude comparison
    is fair because the set was built from parent comments, which neither side contains.
  - Sentence splitting is by . ! ? and newlines; lists and code in HN comments blur it. A first word with a capital
    inside it (iOS, jemalloc-style names) is not counted as a lowercase start. Quoted lines (>) and URLs are removed.
  - Dictionary: /usr/share/dict/words (present). Its misses are mostly names and newer words, per the hand check.
