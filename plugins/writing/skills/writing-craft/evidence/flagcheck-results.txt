Reading: Claude's default flourish is almost always caught. Writing under the skill lowers the catch rate but leaves it
far above the human false-positive rate. The quoted giveaways moved from lyrical turns to contrived quirky specifics
("a dead wife's gloves", "smelled of wet iron", "depth of a spoon", "ate the wreath"): the instruction to be odd and
concrete became a recognisable tic of its own. Confounds: model lines ran ~10 words longer than the human ones; the
human lines are period prose (the period condition narrows but does not remove that); self-preference does not apply
(Gemini grading Claude). Human readers are the next check.
First of two blind human raters (scripts/quiz_score.py): 20/30; human lines called human 9/10, Claude default
called machine 6/10, Claude under the skill called machine 5/10 (chance; Gemini caught the same set at 81%). One rater,

Second rater: 26/30; human called human 6/10, Claude default
caught 10/10, Claude skill caught 10/10. The "wrong"
human calls were period purple prose judged to be confused metaphor (a flower image whose referent was unclear):
modern readers read ornate prose as AI whoever wrote it, and the pre-1929 control set is florid, which inflates false
positives. The human lines called AI were, on a second look, good trap sentences, of the kind models
imitate. Pre-1919 Gutenberg books are a standard training corpus (PG-19, part of The Pile), so a florid model voice and
Edwardian purple prose plausibly share ancestry; how heavily any lab weights them is not public. Consequence: old
public-domain prose is a muddied control for flourish (the model is partly being compared with its own sources), and
readers conflate "bad prose" with "AI prose". The modern control (scripts/modern/, 2018 Reddit fiction) addresses it.
