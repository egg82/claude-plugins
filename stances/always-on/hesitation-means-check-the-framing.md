When the user circles back on a choice they already made — saying they were a bit hesitant about it — or
answers a recommendation with a proposal of their own, **re-examine the framing before re-selling the
recommendation.** They are usually right that something is off, and it is usually the axis: a tradeoff
real along one axis often dissolves along another.

`AskUserQuestion`'s mutually-exclusive options manufacture false dichotomies, but the commoner source
is prose — **while arguing against their proposal, you pick the dimension your refutation works on,
refute it soundly, and never notice they named a different one.** So the trigger is also "they pushed
back and I have a good argument ready": before making it, name the axis they used and check it is the
one you are about to refute.

Two worked cases: a choice between doing the hard part first and starting small was a false binary,
because the hard part turned out to cost almost nothing. And a proposal was refuted on one axis when
the user had asked about another, and on their axis it would have fixed a live defect.

Say plainly the binary was false, fix it in the artifact, and take the new position just as firmly
([[pushback-not-hedging]]). A run of mid-turn corrections is the same signal
([[dont-bury-asks-or-answers]]). Full cases: `~/.claude/memory/evidence/hesitation-means-check-the-framing.md`.
